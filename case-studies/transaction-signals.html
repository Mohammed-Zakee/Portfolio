01 / THE QUESTION

A useful alert needs a useful explanation.

In a review workflow, flagging everything creates work and flagging nothing misses unusual activity. This experiment explores that trade-off without using customer information or pretending to make a financial decision.

What was built for this portfolio

A deterministic synthetic data generator, a statistical scoring baseline, evaluation metrics, an adjustable threshold, and CSV export. This is a new demonstration added in November 2026, separate from my employment projects.

02 / METHOD

Simple enough to inspect.

  1. Generate 400 ordinary training records using fixed seed 104729. Features are an amount and a recent transaction count.
  2. Fit means and population standard deviations to log(1 + amount) and transaction count using only the training set.
  3. Generate 200 separate evaluation records using seed 130363 and a 12% anomaly probability. The observed number of anomalies can differ from 24.
  4. Score each feature as its positive deviation from the training mean, divided by the training standard deviation. The transaction score is the larger feature score.
  5. Flag scores at or above the chosen threshold. Compare flags with generated labels to compute precision and recall.

Higher amounts or bursts can increase a score. A high score explains deviation from this generator’s baseline; it does not establish fraud. Negative deviations are deliberately ignored by this one-sided baseline.

03 / REPRODUCIBLE RESULTS

The default baseline.

At a threshold of 2.0 standard deviations: 17 true positives, 0 false positives, 5 false negatives, and 178 true negatives .

Precision: 100.0% . Recall: 77.3% . Evaluation size: 200 synthetic records. Recomputed from the shipped scoring code on 14 November 2026.

These are measurements on a toy generator, not evidence of performance on real payments. The generator intentionally overlaps classes, but it also creates the patterns the detector looks for. That makes this evaluation easier than an independently collected dataset.

Try thresholds 0.5 and 4.5 above to inspect how alert volume and missed anomalies change. Download the full scored set to check the confusion counts yourself.

04 / LIMITATIONS & NEXT STEPS

A baseline, with clear boundaries.

  • Synthetic labels are generator choices, not investigated fraud outcomes. No real-world accuracy, business savings, or production readiness is claimed.
  • No per-customer history, geography, merchant context, class-drift handling, or calibrated fraud probabilities are modeled.
  • The evaluation set is visible in this demo. Adjusting the threshold against it is exploration, not a valid final model-selection procedure.
  • Next: use a separately held-out, appropriately licensed dataset, choose thresholds on validation data, compare with a trained anomaly detector, and measure review capacity and false-positive costs.

This demonstration flags synthetic records for review; it does not approve, block, or score real people or payments.
